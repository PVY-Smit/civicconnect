| **Each form of verification evidence proves something the others cannot.** Unit tests isolate a unit and cannot show integrated behaviour, API and integration tests expose a broken authorisation check at the endpoint but cannot show behaviour under load, and static analysis recognises patterns without judging intent, addressing around sixteen percent of the issues manual reviewers identify (Singh et al., 2017, cited in Yang et al., 2026) | Verification effort spread evenly rather than by risk, so the requirements that matter most carry no deeper evidence than the rest | A risk-to-verification map, as in Figure 1, tracing each risk by likelihood and impact to the evidence best placed to expose it, and stating what that evidence cannot prove | The three forms overlap only partially, so a gap remains wherever no form was assigned, and a risk rated too low receives too little evidence | Which requirements carry risk severe enough to need evidence beyond the NFR-012 minimum of automated tests passing on every pull request, and what that additional evidence is for each |
| **Testing can show the presence of defects and never their absence** (Dijkstra, 1972), so a fully passing pipeline shows only that the configured checks found nothing in the code they examined at the time they ran | A green pipeline being read as proof that the product is ready, when it reports only the checks that ran | Quality gates whose response follows the evidence behind them: an absolute block where the evidence is close to exhaustive within its scope, such as negative access tests, and a threshold where it samples a moving target, such as performance and dependency advisories (Figure 1) | A gate is only as good as the checks configured into it, and each check has a boundary past which it says nothing, so the gate cannot show that the requirements themselves were right | What the team must be able to show, besides passing checks, before a requirement is recorded as verified in the RTM, and what #67's checks are therefore allowed to claim |
| **Access-control failures are the most frequently occurring category in the data behind the OWASP Top 10:2025, and the guidance is to deny by default and validate permissions on every request** (OWASP, 2025a; OWASP, no date b) | Unauthorised access to records, and elevation of privilege where a check exists only in the interface | A single policy component consulted by every protected operation, with negative tests for each role and function pair executed against the endpoints rather than through the interface | The tests cover only the matrix that was written. A function added later carries no evidence until the matrix is extended, so the assurance decays silently as the system grows | Which functions and record scopes form the matrix that negative tests are generated from, and what keeps that matrix current as endpoints are added |
| **A credential that reaches the repository stays in its history after deletion, and OWASP treats rotation and revocation as part of the secret lifecycle rather than as incident cleanup** (OWASP, no date c) | Credential exposure through source, configuration or build output | Secrets injected from outside source control, scanning on every push together with a full-history scan, and a rotation procedure that has been exercised at least once | A scan detects recognisable patterns. An unusual format or a secret inside a built image can pass, and a clean scan cannot show that a secret was never exposed | What secret-handling evidence has to exist before a release is acceptable, and who holds and exercises the rotation procedure |
| **Automated and AI-assisted security work carries measured error: approximately 40 percent of 1,689 generated programs were vulnerable across 89 scenarios, and users with an AI assistant wrote less secure code while being more likely to believe it secure** (Pearce et al., 2022; Perry et al., 2023) | Over-reliance on tool output or generated code as security evidence | Treating scanner and assistant output as a lead that requires independent verification, and directing human review at the design and authorisation questions tools cannot judge | Human review is weak in this area on its own, since only about one percent of review comments address security (Bacchelli and Bird, 2013 and di Biase et al., 2016, both cited in Yang et al., 2026) | What verification must accompany AI-assisted or scanner-cleared security work before it is accepted into the baseline, and who is accountable for that verification |
| **Uncontrolled differences between environments let defects appear only after deployment** (Wiggins, 2017a), **and a successful build shows only that an artefact can be produced** | Behaviour that differs between a developer machine and the deployed environment, which RSK-13 scores at 12 and FEC-05 records as an open parity question | Documented and intentional environment differences, deploy-specific configuration held outside source (Wiggins, 2017b), and a versioned artefact deployed through a repeatable procedure to a representative staging environment, followed by post-deployment smoke checks (Microsoft, 2026b) | Automation is not proof of safety, since an automated process can repeatedly apply an incorrect configuration, and parity does not require identical capacity, credentials or data, so some differences remain by design | Which environments the project will actually have, what parity between them is guaranteed rather than assumed, and what evidence of a repeatable deployment exists before a release is accepted |
| **Latency, traffic, errors and saturation are four important monitoring signals for a user-facing system** (Ewaschuk, 2016), **and telemetry has value only when it is interpreted against workload health and turned into meaningful alerts** (Microsoft, 2026a) | Operational failure going unnoticed, where NFR-003 commits to 98 percent availability across published service hours measured by an external probe against a health endpoint | Defined health indicators for the four signals, alert conditions that name who responds, and logs on important paths that are used during diagnosis | More telemetry improves diagnosis while adding storage and operational overhead, and an alert nobody can act on provides little value (Microsoft, 2025a) | What the health endpoint reports, what is monitored beyond availability, and who acts on an alert, given that the baseline commits nobody to being on call and CON-03 rules out paid tooling |
